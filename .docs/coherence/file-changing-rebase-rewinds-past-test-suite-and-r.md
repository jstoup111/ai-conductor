## Consistency and achievability adjudication

All 28 criteria have named production or lower-layer mechanisms in the cited Done-when blocks. The cross-layer sweep found no unresolved contradictions after the approved conflict resolutions and the following completion-check clarifications. These clarify existing obligations; they do not change accepted behavior or the nineteen-task dependency graph.

- Story 6 interrupted persistence: Task 10's reconciliation promise needed a mandatory consumer-side publication fence. Added Task 8's explicit applying/inconsistent-operation check, including otherwise-passing old verdicts. Task 9 now also requires verdict/state agreement before marking an operation applied. These directly implement new ADR D5 and prevent partial-write preservation from being accepted through a legacy fallback.
- Intake outcome 1: added Task 11's explicit test_suite-or-later selected continuation check; coverage refresh remains in place, as already approved in ADR D4 and Story 3.
- Several architecture decisions span multiple task checks. Expanded their operative task citations and retained the original mappings beside their amendments. The existing original-judge stamping obligation was corrected to existing with verified stampCode/writeGateCodeStamp/stampGateRunIdentity evidence.

The unchanged/default cases were checked against every new side effect on their path: skipped gates acquire no new gate effect; unrelated documents do not create a competing entry/collision action; an actual completed relevant rebase may write its own operation evidence but does not reopen unrelated lifecycle steps. Events identify applied effects, not candidate effects. Repetition may report the same operation but cannot claim another reopening or issue another convergence credit; exactly-once event transport is not an accepted requirement.

The classifier's unchanged/changed/unproved replay values describe only comparison results. Entry skip/noop, disabled gates, protected refusal, coverage refusal, infrastructure failure, and applying-operation recovery retain their distinct existing/result contexts rather than being falsely represented as a successful unchanged replay. Task 1 owns raw driver identity capture; Tasks 2–3 validate Git reconstruction; Task 11 proves those paths reach actual conductor decisions. Tasks 6–8 connect raw persisted gate bindings to shared validity and real consumers; Task 10 owns interrupted writes and Task 8 their publication fence. The detailed invalid-input permutations stay at their lowest sufficient layer.

Repair and preservation cannot oscillate under the stated checks: Tasks 5 and 7 reject outstanding ordinary failures; Task 15 makes a real suite failure supersede old preservation before normal repair; Tasks 8 and 10 refuse unfinished application. Conversely a missing BUILD completion proof takes Task 14's recovery path rather than inventing a repair task. Infrastructure and coverage failures retain their own owners and cannot masquerade as test failures.

Existing/no-change architecture evidence was checked in source: artifacts.ts owns stampCode, writeGateCodeStamp, stampGateRunIdentity, and checkStepCompletion; conductor.ts's post-rebase preVerify uses native suite CURRENT/PRESERVED_WITHIN_BUDGET inspection and BUILD derivation; the state port retains expected-value batches and the declared tree-attesting set remains BUILD/test_suite. No new gate is declared tree-attesting; no configuration/CLI/hook change is required.

The two prerequisite PRs remain separate implementation owners. Their required delivery is a dispatch dependency to establish before handoff, not a promise proven by a unit test. Normal-finish/re-kick policy and protected/collision recovery are tested at their actual caller boundaries. No production task targets another feature's DECIDE artifact.

Result: five outcomes, seven stories, nineteen tasks, five ADR files (23 citable decisions), and 28 criteria are covered. Technical track omits FR rows.
