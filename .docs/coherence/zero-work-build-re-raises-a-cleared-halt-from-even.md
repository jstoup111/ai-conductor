# Coherence: BUILD settles without a dispatch when no work is owed

Source-Ref: jstoup111/ai-conductor#3659. Tier M, technical track (no PRD, so no `fr` rows).

Outcome mapping: outcome 1 is Stories 1 and 2: a BUILD with no work owed settles without a provider session at the dispatch boundary, including on a revalidate round. Outcome 2 is Story 4, with Stories 1 and 2 removing the dispatch in which the observed re-raise happened: the stall remediation is told which halt reasons were cleared and judges whether live state still supports the question. Outcome 3 is Story 3: a confirmed `no-work-owed` verdict settles BUILD with no HALT, and a contradicted one resumes BUILD under the existing remediation round budget. Outcome 4 is Story 5. Outcome 5 is the negative paths of Stories 1, 2 and 3.

Operator decision on outcome 4 (2026-11-15): the operator declined a new command. The supported path is the existing halt clear command followed by the next dispatch, which settles BUILD. Story 5 proves that path through the production halt clear function.

Operator decision on outcome 3 (2026-11-15): the outcome allows a mechanical halt as the fallback. The approved design uses no new HALT: a contradicted verdict resumes BUILD with the pending work named, and exhaustion of the existing budget halts as it does today.

Achievability pass: every criterion row cites a Done-when check asserted at the criterion's own layer: the conductor's step loop, its stall branch, the stored verdict, the event ledger, the remediation dispatch context, the plan contract, or the policy text. Tasks 1, 2, 3 and 15 are lower-layer infrastructure; Tasks 4, 13 and 16 carry them to the conductor. The verdict outcome set gains `no-work-owed` with two origins, which represent the two settle paths; every negative path ends in an outcome that already exists (a dispatch, an existing guard's HALT, the planner-fault outcome, or a resume). No criterion requires violating a decision of either ADR in the change set.
