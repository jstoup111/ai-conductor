## Consistency and achievability judgment

Read failure versus optional logs: Tasks 1–2 decode required source data; Task 11 refuses unusable required context and preserves net attempts; Task 3 degrades optional logs while retaining useful metadata. Task 12 emits the corresponding root-bus diagnostic even on early refusal. Required refusal therefore cannot accidentally be treated as optional degradation, and optional failure does not create a blind repair.

Metadata completeness versus budgets: Story 2 explicitly permits bounded omissions with markers. Tasks 3 and 11 bound bytes, entries, requests and captured subprocess output while preserving useful identities before logs. Story 1's identity/link assertions apply to retained context, with Story 2 governing oversized inputs; a truncated or absent URL cannot be invented as a valid link. A mixed rollup uses the typed source boundary owned by Tasks 1 and 11, not a fabricated helper-only shape.

Build inheritance versus readiness: Task 6 reaches the real shared executor with native model/effort and fresh sessions; Task 11 removes the independent startup veto. Tasks 4–5 preserve a direct refusal only when no candidate may have executed. Inconclusive Codex readiness proceeds under the existing policy; auth/permission and ordinary failures keep their classifications. The final all-unavailable result and model fallback cannot erase an earlier attempt. No task gives telemetry, onSpawn or a missing custom-provider probe refund authority.

Reservation versus refund: Task 10 reserves before dispatch and restores count/cooldown only with direct no-start/branch-gone evidence. Tasks 4–6 and 11 establish how such evidence reaches that boundary. Noop, failure, publication, thrown and ambiguous results retain one reservation; only later remote green resets it. Failure-detection state survives refunds. This satisfies both limited attempts and no charges for proven refusals without an oscillating reset/refund policy.

Verification versus publication: Task 7 requires a successful session and changed committed HEAD. Task 8 owns guard and configured-verifier refusal; Task 9 owns lease-protected publication. Task 11 forwards their results and Task 10 keeps them distinct from green CI. The legal final results represent no-start, noop, failed stage, published and branch-gone; no required scenario is forced into an untruthful success state. Provider instructions retain daemon ownership of verifier and push.

Preserved paths and side effects: Task 10's draft, conflicting, pending/running, cooldown, sticky, disabled and serial protections forbid a new repair/attempt; they do not promise zero existing labeling or failure diagnostics. Task 12's diagnostics are bounded, best-effort observations and cannot relax those gates or change outcomes. Shared tracker options retain defaults for unrelated callers. No provider-specific readiness requirement is added for custom providers, and neither unrelated lifecycle nor rebase authority changes.
